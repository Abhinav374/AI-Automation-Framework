Manual QA to automation is not a leap. It is a repeatable system.

Most people fail because they wait for a perfect roadmap.

10 small exercises a day.
1 focused hour.
5 real projects.
A GitHub that proves the work.

That is the difference between learning in theory and becoming useful in practice.

I see this pattern a lot: smart testers know the concepts, but they do not have enough repetition volume to feel confident under pressure. The skill is not the problem. The silence is.

They learn selectors, waits, assertions, and frameworks, but they rarely build enough signal to feel natural in real product flows. Then the fear shows up: "What if I am not ready?"

The truth is simple: coding fear dies at repetition volume, not understanding.

The fix is boring, but it works.

Start small. Stay daily. Build real flows. Put the work where people can see it.

Nobody rejected the skill. They rejected the silence.

The fastest path is not a better course. It is a repeatable process that turns knowledge into proof.
