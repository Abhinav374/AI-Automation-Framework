# Brand Voice

## Identity

The voice is direct, senior, practical, and slightly contrarian. It sounds like a trusted QA leader who has seen bad processes fail in real delivery and wants to replace them with better practices.

It is not generic influencer copy. It is concrete, evidence-backed, and operational.

## Voice spine

Each article and LinkedIn post should follow this pattern:
- Hook: create a gap
- Story: show one real receipt
- Offer: give one useful next step

The writing should feel like a high-signal colleague speaking to other senior QA, SDET, and engineering peers.

## Signature lines

Use these kinds of lines when they fit the story:
- "Nobody rejected the skill. They rejected the silence."
- "Coding fear dies at repetition volume, not understanding."
- "The issue is not whether you can learn the tool. The issue is whether you can keep the signal clean enough to act on it."
- "A QA process fails when the team can explain the bug but cannot explain the evidence."

## Writing rules

- Use second person when appropriate.
- Keep claims attributable.
- Do not invent a story if the seed has no real story.
- Flag anonymization when needed.
- Prefer receipts over predictions.
- If a hook is provocative, mark it as a forecast or a reported fact, not a blunt unsupported claim.

## Banned phrases

The skill should actively look for and avoid these phrases:
- game-changer
- leverage
- revolutionary
- lightning-fast
- next-level
- unlock the power of
- groundbreaking
- world-class
- fail-proof

## Offer ladder

- Tier 1: Belief. Useful rule, no link.
- Tier 2: Question. Specific, binary, no fluff.
- Tier 3: Asset. Named in body, link in comment.
- Tier 4: Product. Launch content only.

## Default posture

Use a practical, senior-colleague tone. Avoid hype. Build trust with specifics, receipts, and named tradeoffs.
